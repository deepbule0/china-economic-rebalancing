# China’s Trade Surplus, the Transition Beyond Land Finance, and Household Rebalancing

[中文](analysis.zh-CN.md) · [Project overview](README.md) · [Sources](SOURCES.md)

*Discussion summary • 13 October 2026*  
*Perspective: China’s long-term economic interests. Recommendations and forecasts are analytical judgments.*

## 1. Core argument

China’s large external surplus, weak household demand, and the decline of land-based local-government finance are connected. A durable adjustment requires **stronger household purchasing power and a fiscal system that can fund public services without relying on continually rising property values**.

The goal is a sustainable external balance alongside strong manufacturing, reliable public services, and higher household welfare. Fiscal reform and immediate household support can proceed together.

## 2. What the surplus and land-revenue figures mean

| Indicator | Figure | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customs goods-trade surplus, 2025 | About **US$1.189 trillion** [1] | An annual export–import flow, not government profit or an equivalent increase in official reserves. |
| Current-account surplus, 2025 | **US$735 billion** [2] | Includes services and income flows; customs and balance-of-payments goods statistics also differ. |
| Land-use-right transfer revenue, 2021 | **RMB8.705 trillion** [3] | Gross revenue in the government-managed funds budget. |
| Land-use-right transfer revenue, 2025 | **RMB4.152 trillion** [4] | About **52.3% below 2021**, calculated from the two final-account totals. |

Land revenue belongs to a separate budget from the general public budget. Gross receipts also fund land-related costs, including acquisition and compensation. The decline is therefore not an equal-sized loss of freely spendable tax revenue.

The accounting identity **current account = national saving − domestic investment** describes the domestic counterpart of the external balance; it does not establish the causes of high saving by itself.

Foreign claims can be held by firms, banks, investors, and monetary authorities. They are not all dollar assets or official reserves. A weaker dollar reduces the RMB value of unhedged dollar holdings, but total returns also depend on interest, asset prices, and import purchasing power. **Portfolio diversification manages asset risk; fiscal and household reform address the domestic imbalance.**

A surplus can finance future imports and support an ageing population. The concern is excessive reliance on foreign demand, exposure to trade retaliation, and whether domestic welfare is being sacrificed to accumulate foreign claims.

## 3. Why the local tax and land-finance problem matters

Our interpretation is that declining land revenue can reinforce export dependence through two channels:

- **Demand pressure:** spending restraint, delayed payments to contractors, and uncertainty about public services can weaken household and business income and increase precautionary saving.
- **Production incentives:** localities may compete for factories and industrial investment to rebuild employment and tax revenue, even when additional capacity has weak returns.

These mechanisms vary by region. Productivity, foreign demand, exchange rates, and supply chains also matter. Nevertheless, the fiscal issue is more than replacing land receipts: it is matching spending responsibilities with sustainable revenue and improving incentives to support residents and services.

China’s consumption tax is a **selective excise**, not a general retail sales tax. Moving its collection downstream and allocating more revenue to local governments would not automatically create a destination-based tax-sharing system. The detailed allocation formula matters.

## 4. Historical lessons

| Experience | Lesson |
| --- | --- |
| China after the global financial crisis | Investment-linked imports helped reduce the external surplus, alongside terms-of-trade and other changes. A lower surplus can coexist with greater domestic dependence on investment. [5] |
| Japan after the 1985 Plaza Accord | Appreciation, monetary easing, lending, and asset-market dynamics interacted. Japan’s bubble had several causes; appreciation alone is not a sufficient explanation. [6] |

The analytical implication is to avoid relying exclusively on either a property-and-infrastructure investment surge or abrupt currency appreciation.

## 5. Recommended policy package

1. **Protect local services and clear arrears.** Strengthen predictable transfers and central or provincial funding for basic services. Resolve unsustainable debts alongside controls on new borrowing.
2. **Raise household income and security.** Improve basic pensions, healthcare, unemployment protection, childcare support, and migrant workers’ access to services. Fund benefits reliably so households can reduce precautionary saving.
3. **Build a stable local revenue system.** Implement appropriate consumption-tax reforms and review shared-tax allocation. Transfer formulas should reflect population, expenditure needs, and regional fiscal capacity.
4. **Align spending responsibilities with funding.** Consider greater central or provincial responsibility for nationally desirable minimum service standards while retaining effective local delivery and accountability.
5. **Sequence new taxes carefully.** A recurrent property tax could offer a long-term local base, but housing conditions, exemptions, valuation, and household cash flow matter. Broad new burdens on ordinary households could undermine the demand objective.
6. **Manage external adjustment gradually.** Expand commercially useful imports and domestic services; allow exchange-rate flexibility; diversify overseas assets according to returns, liquidity, and risk.

Outbound investment changes the financing or investment of a surplus; it **does not directly reduce the current-account surplus**. RMB settlement changes currency exposure, not the saving–investment balance. Higher household spending would also not reduce the surplus one-for-one: domestic output, investment, and imports would respond.

## 6. Announced reform versus analytical proposals

| Measure | Status or qualification |
| --- | --- |
| Broader local tax sources, improved transfers, downstream consumption-tax reform, and increased central spending responsibilities | Reform directions in the **2024 Third Plenum decision**; direction does not imply complete implementation. [7] |
| Local hidden-debt resolution | The 2024 framework combined **RMB6 trillion** of additional local debt limits with **RMB4 trillion** from special-bond arrangements over five years. These are multi-year debt-resolution resources, not RMB10 trillion of new household transfers. [8] |
| Central assumption of substantial local debt | A possible further reform. Existing swaps generally replace hidden liabilities with formal **local-government bonds**, rather than automatically moving the debt to the central government. [8] |
| Destination-oriented shared-tax allocation and a gradual recurrent property tax | Proposals in this analysis, not claims of completed nationwide reforms. |

Debt swaps can lower financing costs and ease repayment pressure. They cannot permanently repair a recurring mismatch between spending obligations and sustainable revenue.

## 7. Who approves what?

The process is not a universal rule that every important policy requires a separate NPC vote. The Constitution assigns the NPC plan and budget approval powers and the State Council executive responsibilities. [9]

| Institution | Main role |
| --- | --- |
| CPC leadership, including the Politburo and central economic-policy meetings | Sets strategic and political priorities. |
| State Council and ministries | Develop policy proposals; the State Council submits national plan and budget proposals to the NPC. |
| Full NPC | Examines and approves the national development plan and central budget, alongside reviewing the national budget report; exercises its legislative powers. |
| NPC Standing Committee | Exercises legislative and oversight powers and approves necessary plan/budget adjustments within its authority while the NPC is not sitting. |
| State Council | Decides programs and administrative measures within its legal and fiscal authority. |
| MoF, NDRC, PBOC, NFRA and other ministries/agencies | Develop detailed rules, allocate authorized funds, and supervise execution according to their responsibilities. |
| Local governments and financial institutions | Deliver services, payments, and financial programs. |

The annual budget is not an unrestricted pool. Spending must respect authorized purposes and budget rules; statutory adjustment conditions require the prescribed approval procedure. Local budgets are approved by the corresponding local people’s congresses. [10]

**Five-Year Plan:** CPC recommendations → State Council/NDRC draft the Outline → NPC approval → publication and implementation. The NPC approved the **15th Five-Year Plan Outline on 12 March 2026**. Its strategic objectives do not, by themselves, specify every annual appropriation or benefit formula. [11]

**Industrial policies:** many large programs can be launched by the State Council and ministries inside existing law, plans, and funding authority. If a policy needs new legislation, additional debt authority, or a statutory budget adjustment, legislative approval becomes relevant.

**Tariffs:** the NPC Standing Committee passed the Tariff Law in 2024. It assigns tariff responsibilities to the State Council Tariff Commission, with specified measures requiring State Council approval. Customs collects the duties. There is no new full-NPC vote for every tariff change; executive discretion is bounded by the law. [12]

## 8. Case study: consumption-loan interest subsidies

The original rollout illustrates how a broad fiscal commitment becomes a usable program. The table records the **2025 launch and January 2026 revision**, rather than a complete guide to current eligibility.

| Date | Step |
| --- | --- |
| March 2025 | The budget report already identified interest subsidies for personal consumption loans in key areas. [13] |
| 16 March 2025 | The central consumption action plan announced subsidies for eligible personal-consumption and service-business loans. [14] |
| 31 July 2025 | The State Council executive meeting deployed the programs. [15] |
| 4 August 2025; public release 12 August | MoF, PBOC and NFRA issued the original implementation plan. [16] |
| 1 September 2025 | The program began operating. [16] |
| 16 January 2026; public release 19 January | Revised rules extended the program through 31 December 2026, included credit-card bill installments, and removed consumption-category restrictions. [17] |

The original plan specified a **one-percentage-point annual subsidy**, capped at 50% of the contract rate, an original **RMB3,000 cumulative cap per borrower per institution**, and **90% central / 10% provincial financing**. Banks deducted eligible subsidies from interest charged and sought fiscal reimbursement through pre-allocation and settlement. These were executive implementation details. [16]

For illustration, a qualifying RMB100,000 balance outstanding for a full year at 3% interest would incur RMB3,000 interest; a one-point subsidy would reduce the borrower’s cost by RMB1,000, subject to the applicable rules.

The lesson is **budget authority and spending purpose first; program specifications and delivery mechanisms through the executive system**. The NPC did not separately vote on every cap, bank, or transaction category.

## 9. Where does a stronger household-policy shift stand?

“Stage 3” is our discussion label for stronger, sustained support for household income and security. It is **not an official stage or an announced package**.

The cited documents establish several distinct facts:

- The national Five-Year Plan Outline has already passed the NPC. [11]
- The State Council’s approval of the consumption-specific Five-Year Plan is dated **2 July 2026**. This sectoral plan is distinct from the national Outline. [18]
- MoF held a **2027 central-department budget preparation meeting on 3 June 2026**. This confirms budget preparation, not approval of a particular household package. [19]
- The October 2026 fiscal report describes existing consumption and welfare measures already in execution. Household support is therefore not wholly waiting at the proposal stage. [20]

**Analytical judgment:** the strategic direction is established, existing measures are being implemented, and annual budget preparation is underway. Those facts do not establish the size or adoption date of a single future large redistribution package.

Two routes remain possible:

| Route | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Annual budget | Political priorities → State Council budget proposal → NPC approval → program rules and execution. |
| Measures during the year | Executive decisions within existing authority and funds; statutory budget or debt changes go through the relevant legislative procedure. |

Late-2026 economic-policy meetings and the **2027 NPC budget process** are useful checkpoints. Expectations about their significance are forecasts; this summary does not assert an exact future meeting date, guaranteed benefit increase, or predetermined redistribution amount.

## 10. What would demonstrate meaningful progress?

Watch **actual budget allocations and execution**, not only policy language: predictable local-service funding, tax-sharing changes, arrears clearance, additional funded welfare, and limits on renewed off-budget debt. Compare recurring household benefits with loan subsidies and one-off purchases separately.

Judge rebalancing by household real disposable income, consumption, service reliability, and the current account. A larger headline deficit or more overseas investment alone would not demonstrate that the underlying problem has been resolved.
